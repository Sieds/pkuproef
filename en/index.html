What is PKU?

PKU stands for phenylketonuria, and is a rare, incurable, inherited metabolic disorder that occurs in about 1 in 18,000 newborns. If you have PKU, your liver is not able to process one specific component of proteins. That culprit, the amino acid phenylalanine (Phe), therefore accumulates in your blood and hinders the growth and development of the brain. Untreated, PKU leads to brain damage. In most countries, newborns are checked for PKU with the heel prick. If PKU is detected in a baby, treatment is started immediately.

That treatment should prevent the accumulation of the amino acid phenylalanine in the body. But that amino acid is a natural part of almost all food. This is why PKU patients follow a strict low protein diet throughout their lives. No meat, no fish, no cereals, no milk, no soy and no nuts, but a lot of fruit and vegetables.

Because you get too few nutrients in this diet, PKU patients take a special Phe-free preparation several times a day, which contains all the missing amino acids and minerals. PKU patients are under the strict supervision of a dietitian, regularly go to the hospital and regularly send blood from their finger (auch!) to check their protein housekeeping.

If you follow the diet and take your preparations, you can lead a relatively 'normal' life. More information about the medical side of PKU and new developments can be found on the website of the PKU Vereniging (Dutch PKU Association).

About the cookbooks

The first PKU cookbook contains 101 low-protein recipes. In the cookbook you will find soups, side dishes, salads, pizzas, pasta and rice dishes, stews and curries, potato dishes, vegetable dishes, snacks, desserts and pastries. Half of the recipes have a tasty photo showing the end result.

Our second cookbook contains 80 recipes with once again - at the request of our supporters - a photo of the dish with every recipe. In the book you will find starters, main courses, meat and fish substitutes, side dishes, desserts, breakfast & lunch dishes, savoury and sweet snacks and biscuits & pastries.

All recipes provide a step-by-step description of how to prepare the dish. Sometimes useful tips are given, so that you can easily vary. Of course, each dish contains a list of the ingredients you need, with the associated protein and Phenyl values. This makes it easy to incorporate dishes into your diet. These overviews are also useful if you want to leave out ingredients because you don't like them. Or if you want to add things and vary. Most of the ingredients are readily available in the supermarket, sometimes combined with special low-protein products (such as pasta and flour) that you can usually easily order online.

For determining the protein values, we used various reputable sources, the most important being voedingswaardetabel.nl and nevo-online.rivm.nl. Both books have been reviewed by dietitians specialised in PKU.
